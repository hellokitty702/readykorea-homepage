Your first week in a Korean kindergarten or hagwon moves fast. Students test boundaries, co-teachers watch how you manage energy, and you are still learning names. The goal is not a perfect lesson — it is a calm, repeatable routine.

Day 1–2: Lock the opening routine

Use the same 5-minute opener every class: greeting → name check → one movement song. Familiar structure lowers anxiety for young learners and gives you breathing room.

  • Keep instructions short: “Stand up. Listen. Repeat.”
  • Use the same seating signal every time.
  • Write target language on the board before students enter.

Day 3–5: Teach one clear target

Pick one language goal per class (for example: “Hello / How are you?”). Overloading week one creates noise. One strong target with a game, a chant, and a quick exit ticket is enough.

Energy management

Korean young-learner classes often run hot. Alternate high-energy TPR with quiet table work. If the room spikes, reset with a silent countdown and a known chant instead of raising your voice.
