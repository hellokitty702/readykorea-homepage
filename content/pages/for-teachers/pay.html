5. Severance, Pension & Pay — Know Your Rights

Foreigners have the SAME labor rights as Koreans by law

One of the most expensive misconceptions: many foreign teachers think their contract is their rights. Korean labor law overrides contracts. Even if your contract excludes severance, you\'re legally entitled. Here\'s exactly what you should get.

💰 Severance pay (퇴직금)

  • Statutory right under Korean Labor Standards Act — you get it whether contract mentions it or not
  • Amount: ~1 month\'s salary per year of service
  • Requires 12+ months continuous employment
  • Must be paid within 14 days of contract end
  • Scam alert: 11-month terminations — some hagwons fire teachers at 11 months to avoid the 12-month trigger. If termination appears designed to evade severance, you can sue.

🏦 National Pension Service (국민연금)

  • Enrollment mandatory for E-2 visa holders (with some exceptions)
  • You contribute 4.5% of salary · Employer contributes 4.5% · Total 9%
  • Lump-sum refund available when you leave Korea (covers both your + employer contributions + interest)
  • Cannot refund if you\'re from a country that signed pension agreement (UK, etc.) — check your country
  • Australians, Canadians, Americans usually get full refund

🏥 National Health Insurance (NHI)

  • Mandatory enrollment for E-2 holders (since 2019)
  • Cost: ~7% of salary, split 50/50 with employer
  • Co-pay: typically 20–30% at clinics, 10–20% at hospitals
  • Standard doctor visit: ~₩7,000–₩10,000 (~$5–$8 USD) with NHI
  • Dental cleaning ~$20 · ER visit ~$50 · MRI ~$150 (your share after NHI)

📞 If pay/severance disputes arise

  • Labor Standards Office (고용노동부) hotline 1350 — English available, free
  • File written complaint at local Labor Office — 60 days to resolve
  • If unpaid wages: small claims court is free + foreigner-friendly
  • Document everything: pay stubs, contracts, written communications
  • Korean labor inspectors often side with foreign teachers when evidence is clear
  • Consider Ask Korea Law for free Q&A
